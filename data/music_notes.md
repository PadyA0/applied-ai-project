# Music Notes

Source corpus for the retrieval layer. Every fun fact the system prints is
copied out of this file, so nothing can be printed that is not written here.

Format: each note starts with `### ` followed by a short title. The lines under
the title are the note body. `src/rag.py` splits on those headings.

## Genre notes

### pop
Pop is less a sound than a chart position. The label follows whatever is selling
at the time, which is why a 1983 pop record and a 2020 pop record can share
almost no instruments.

### rock
Rock built its identity around the electric guitar and the four piece band, and
by the 1970s it had split into so many subgenres that the word mostly signals
attitude rather than a specific sound.

### jazz
Jazz treats the written melody as a starting point rather than the finished
piece. The same tune played by two groups on the same night is expected to come
out different.

### lofi
Lofi keeps the flaws on purpose. Tape hiss, vinyl crackle and slightly loose
timing are added back in, because the imperfections are what make long study
streams feel like a room instead of a recording.

### synthwave
Synthwave started in the 2000s as a tribute to soundtracks from the 1980s, so it
is a modern genre that has always sounded like a memory of a decade most of its
listeners did not live through.

### indie pop
Indie pop borrowed the hooks of pop while keeping the smaller budgets and looser
production of independent labels, which is why the vocals often sit further back
in the mix than a chart record would allow.

### synthpop
Synthpop arrived when synthesizers got cheap enough for bands to buy in the late
1970s. Groups that could not afford a studio full of session players could
suddenly sound enormous.

### disco
Disco is built on a four on the floor kick, one beat on every count. That steady
pulse is what makes it easy to dance to without listening for the beat.

### funk
Funk moves the emphasis to the first beat of the bar, the downbeat that James
Brown called "the One". The groove is carried by the rhythm section rather than
by chord changes.

### soul
Soul grew out of gospel singing moved to secular subjects, which is why the
vocal delivery often sounds like a church performance regardless of the lyrics.

### grunge
Grunge came out of Seattle in the late 1980s around the Sub Pop label, mixing
punk volume with slower tempos and heavy distortion.

### britpop
Britpop was a 1990s British reaction against American grunge, leaning on melody
and local references. The Blur and Oasis chart rivalry made national news.

### metal
Metal is one of the few genres where technical difficulty is openly part of the
appeal. Speed and precision are treated as expressive tools rather than showing
off.

### classical
Classical is the only genre here where the composer and the performer are
usually different people separated by a century or more, so the performance is
an interpretation of a document.

### reggae
Reggae is defined by the "one drop", where the drummer leaves the first beat
empty and lands on the third. That gap is what gives the music its unhurried
feel.

### reggaeton
Reggaeton is built on the dembow rhythm, which travelled from Jamaican dancehall
through Panama to Puerto Rico before becoming a global pop language.

### afrobeats
Afrobeats with an "s" is West African pop from the 2000s onward. It is a
different thing from Fela Kuti's Afrobeat without the "s", which was a 1970s
political funk style, and the two get confused constantly.

### amapiano
Amapiano came out of South Africa in the mid 2010s. Its signature is the log
drum, a deep bending bass sound that carries the melody as much as the rhythm.

### soca
Soca was coined as the "soul of calypso" in Trinidad and Tobago in the 1970s,
speeding calypso up for carnival. Its tempo is built for walking a parade route
for hours.

### bhangra
Bhangra began as a Punjabi harvest dance driven by the double headed dhol drum.
British Asian producers moved it onto club systems in the 1980s.

### k-pop
K-pop treats the music video and choreography as part of the composition rather
than promotion, so songs are often written around what the dance needs.

### j-pop
J-pop is closely tied to anime and game soundtracks, which is why a chart hit
and a series opening theme are frequently the same record.

### flamenco pop
Flamenco pop keeps the palos, the rhythmic patterns of Andalusian flamenco, and
puts modern pop production around them. The handclaps are a structural
instrument, not decoration.

### arabic pop
Arabic pop blends Egyptian and Levantine melodic modes called maqamat with
Western pop arrangements, so the melody can move through intervals a Western
scale does not contain.

### hip hop
Hip hop is the one genre here where the rhythm of the words is the melody. A
verse can carry the hook on its own, which is why the wordiness of a track says
more about it than its tempo does.

### r&b
Contemporary R&B kept soul's vocal approach and moved it onto programmed drums,
so the singing stayed loose while the rhythm underneath became exact.

### country
Country is built around the story. The arrangement usually stays out of the way
because the verse is doing the work, which is why so many country hits survive
being played on one acoustic guitar.

### house
House came out of Chicago clubs in the 1980s, built from a drum machine and a
repeating four on the floor pulse. It was designed for long mixes rather than
for radio, so the arrangements repeat far longer than a pop song would.

## Song and artist notes

### Miles Davis, So What
"So What" opens Kind of Blue from 1959 and is a landmark of modal jazz. It stays
on two scales instead of moving through chord changes, which left the soloists
far more room.

### Dave Brubeck, Take Five
"Take Five" is in 5/4 time, which is why it feels like it is missing a beat. It
was written by the quartet's saxophonist Paul Desmond, not by Brubeck.

### Michael Jackson, Billie Jean
"Billie Jean" came out on Thriller in 1982. Its bassline runs almost unchanged
through the entire song, which is unusual for a record that big.

### Queen, Bohemian Rhapsody
"Bohemian Rhapsody" from 1975 has no chorus. It moves through a ballad, an
operatic section and a hard rock passage instead of repeating a hook.

### Nirvana, Smells Like Teen Spirit
"Smells Like Teen Spirit" opened Nevermind in 1991 and pushed grunge from a
regional Seattle scene onto international charts within months.

### Oasis, Wonderwall
"Wonderwall" appeared on (What's the Story) Morning Glory? in 1995 and became
the Britpop era's most covered song, to the point that pubs banned it.

### Eagles, Hotel California
"Hotel California" from 1976 ends with a two guitar harmony solo that the
players worked out in advance and have reproduced note for note ever since.

### Metallica, Nothing Else Matters
"Nothing Else Matters" came from the 1991 album most people call the Black
Album. It was written as a private piece and almost did not get recorded.

### Stevie Wonder, Superstition
"Superstition" from 1972 is carried by a clavinet, a keyboard with strings
struck by small hammers. Wonder also played the drums on the track.

### Bruno Mars, Uptown Funk
"Uptown Funk" was released in 2014 by Mark Ronson featuring Bruno Mars. It took
months of studio work to sound like a live band playing in one take.

### Daft Punk, Get Lucky
"Get Lucky" from Random Access Memories in 2013 features Nile Rodgers on guitar,
which connects the record directly to the disco era it is referencing.

### The Weeknd, Blinding Lights
"Blinding Lights" runs at roughly 171 beats per minute, far faster than most
chart pop, and leans openly on 1980s synth sounds.

### Billie Eilish, Bad Guy
"Bad Guy" was recorded in a bedroom studio by Eilish and her brother Finneas.
Its restraint stands out because the drop is quieter than the verse.

### Dua Lipa, Levitating
"Levitating" came from Future Nostalgia in 2020, an album built deliberately
around disco and late 1970s dance production.

### Ed Sheeran, Shape of You
"Shape of You" from the 2017 album divide was written with other artists in
mind before Sheeran kept it for himself.

### Adele, Rolling in the Deep
"Rolling in the Deep" opens the album 21 and was written quickly after a
breakup. Adele has described it as a response record rather than a sad one.

### PSY, Gangnam Style
"Gangnam Style" from 2012 was the first video on YouTube to pass one billion
views, and it broke the site's original view counter, which could not hold a
number that large.

### Luis Fonsi, Despacito
"Despacito" was released in 2017 and its remix with Justin Bieber pushed a
Spanish language song to the top of the American chart, which had not happened
in decades.

### Bad Bunny, Titi Me Pregunto
"Titi Me Pregunto" comes from Un Verano Sin Ti in 2022, an album that spent more
weeks at number one in the United States than any other release that year.

### Burna Boy, Last Last
"Last Last" from 2022 is built on a sample of Toni Braxton's "He Wasn't Man
Enough", which places a 2000s American R&B hook inside an afrobeats record.

### Tyla, Water
"Water" from 2023 carried amapiano to a global audience and made Tyla the first
South African solo artist to chart in the American top ten in decades.

### NewJeans, Ditto
"Ditto" leans on a slowed down Baltimore club beat, a regional American dance
style, which is an unusual reference point for a K-pop single.

### YOASOBI, Idol
"Idol" is the opening theme for the anime Oshi no Ko and topped the Japanese
charts in 2023. YOASOBI writes songs by adapting published short stories.

### Koffee, Toast
"Toast" appeared on the Rapture EP. Koffee won the Grammy for Best Reggae Album
with it, becoming the youngest person and the only woman to win that category
on her own.

### Rosalia, Despecha
"Despecha" from 2022 is built on mambo, the Dominican club style, rather than
the flamenco framework Rosalia is usually associated with.

### Machel Montano, Famalay
"Famalay" was recorded with Bunji Garlin and Skinny Fabulous and won the Road
March title for Trinidad Carnival in 2019, meaning it was played more than any
other song along the parade route.

### Amr Diab, Tamally Maak
"Tamally Maak" was released in 2000 and became one of the most widely recognised
Arabic pop songs outside the Arabic speaking world.

### Drake, One Dance
"One Dance" from Views in 2016 features Wizkid and Kyla, and its success is
often credited with pushing afrobeats and UK funky toward mainstream American
radio.

### Claude Debussy, Clair de Lune
"Clair de Lune" is the third movement of the Suite bergamasque. Debussy took the
title from a Verlaine poem, and the piece sat unpublished for about fifteen
years after he wrote it.

### Britney Spears, Toxic
"Toxic" from 2003 is built on a string line lifted from a Bollywood film song,
which is where its unusually sharp, high sound comes from.

### Adele, Someone Like You
"Someone Like You" is voice and piano and nothing else. Adele's label pushed for
a fuller production and she refused, arguing the song would lose its weight.

### Rihanna, Umbrella
"Umbrella" was offered to other artists before Rihanna recorded it in 2007. Its
"ella ella" hook was a studio effect that stayed in because it stuck.

### Harry Styles, As It Was
"As It Was" from 2022 pairs an unusually bright, fast arrangement with lyrics
about separation, which is a large part of why it stayed on charts so long.

### AC/DC, Back in Black
"Back in Black" opened the 1980 album of the same name, the band's first with
Brian Johnson after the death of Bon Scott. It was written as a tribute.

### Led Zeppelin, Stairway to Heaven
"Stairway to Heaven" was never released as a single at the time. Radio played
the album track anyway, and it became one of the most broadcast rock songs ever.

### Foo Fighters, Everlong
"Everlong" came out in 1997 on The Colour and the Shape. Dave Grohl wrote and
recorded much of the band's first material entirely by himself.

### The Killers, Mr Brightside
"Mr Brightside" was one of the first songs the band wrote, and it has spent more
cumulative weeks on the UK singles chart than almost any other record.

### Metallica, Master of Puppets
"Master of Puppets" runs over eight minutes and includes a quiet, clean guitar
section in the middle, which was unusual for thrash metal in 1986.

### Black Sabbath, Paranoid
"Paranoid" was written in a few minutes as filler because the album was running
short. It became the band's best known song.

### System of a Down, Chop Suey
"Chop Suey" from 2001 changes tempo and vocal style repeatedly inside three
minutes, moving from shouted verses to a sung, almost liturgical ending.

### John Coltrane, My Favorite Things
Coltrane took "My Favorite Things" from The Sound of Music and stretched a show
tune into a modal waltz running past thirteen minutes.

### Nina Simone, Feeling Good
"Feeling Good" came from a stage musical before Simone recorded it in 1965. Her
version opens with unaccompanied voice, which was her own decision.

### Frank Sinatra, Fly Me to the Moon
Sinatra's 1964 recording uses a Count Basie arrangement by Quincy Jones. It
travelled with the Apollo programme and was played during the missions.

### Aretha Franklin, Respect
"Respect" was written and first recorded by Otis Redding. Franklin flipped whose
demand it was in 1967, and her version is the one that became an anthem.

### Marvin Gaye, Whats Going On
Motown initially refused to release "What's Going On", believing a protest song
would not sell. Gaye held out until they did.

### Diana Ross, Aint No Mountain High Enough
The song was a duet before Ross recorded her 1970 solo version, which slows it
down and opens with long spoken passages.

### Parliament, Give Up the Funk
Parliament and Funkadelic were largely the same musicians recording under two
names, split so they could release on two labels at once.

### Chic, Le Freak
"Le Freak" started as a song about being refused entry to Studio 54. The chorus
originally used a rather less printable phrase.

### Bee Gees, Stayin Alive
"Stayin' Alive" runs at roughly 103 beats per minute, close to the recommended
rate for CPR chest compressions, and it is taught as a timing mnemonic.

### Gloria Gaynor, I Will Survive
"I Will Survive" was released as a B-side in 1978. Radio DJs flipped the record
over on their own and it became the A-side by public demand.

### ABBA, Dancing Queen
"Dancing Queen" from 1976 is the only ABBA song to reach number one in the
United States, despite the group's enormous success elsewhere.

### The Notorious BIG, Juicy
"Juicy" from 1994 is built on Mtume's "Juicy Fruit" and works as an origin
story, narrating the rise it is simultaneously an example of.

### Eminem, Lose Yourself
"Lose Yourself" was written during filming for 8 Mile and became the first rap
song to win the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

### Kendrick Lamar, Alright
"Alright" from To Pimp a Butterfly in 2015 moved out of the album and into
street protests, where crowds chanted the hook without the record playing.

### TLC, No Scrubs
"No Scrubs" from 1999 gave English a reusable word. "Scrub" as a dismissal
spread well beyond anyone who could name the song.

### Beyonce, Crazy in Love
The horn line in "Crazy in Love" is sampled from the Chi-Lites. Beyonce has said
she disliked the loop at first and had to be talked into keeping it.

### Dolly Parton, Jolene
Parton has said "Jolene" was inspired by a bank teller who flirted with her
husband. The whole song uses only a handful of chords.

### Johnny Cash, Ring of Fire
"Ring of Fire" was written by June Carter, who later married Cash. The mariachi
horns were Cash's idea, reportedly from a dream.

### Bob Marley, Three Little Birds
"Three Little Birds" is widely believed to be about actual birds that sat
outside Marley's window in Kingston.

### Bob Marley, Buffalo Soldier
"Buffalo Soldier" was released in 1983, after Marley's death, and takes its
subject from the Black regiments of the nineteenth century US Army.

### Wizkid, Essence
"Essence" was an album track with no push behind it before listeners found it on
their own, and it became the first Nigerian song to chart in the US top ten.

### Rema, Calm Down
"Calm Down" from 2022 grew far past its original release once a remix with
Selena Gomez opened it to a second audience.

### BTS, Dynamite
"Dynamite" from 2020 was the group's first single recorded entirely in English,
released during a period when touring was impossible.

### BLACKPINK, How You Like That
"How You Like That" set a record for the most views on a YouTube video in the
first twenty four hours after release.

### Mariya Takeuchi, Plastic Love
"Plastic Love" is a 1984 city pop record that was largely forgotten until a
YouTube recommendation algorithm surfaced it to a global audience decades later.

### Kenshi Yonezu, Lemon
"Lemon" was written for a television drama while Yonezu was grieving his
grandfather, and the two subjects ended up inseparable in the lyrics.

### Daddy Yankee, Gasolina
"Gasolina" from 2004 is widely credited with carrying reggaeton out of Puerto
Rico and onto radio worldwide.

### Ludwig van Beethoven, Fur Elise
Nobody is certain who Elise was. The manuscript was found decades after
Beethoven died, and the name may simply be a misreading of his handwriting.

### Antonio Vivaldi, Spring
Vivaldi published "Spring" alongside sonnets describing birdsong, a storm and a
barking dog, and the music follows those texts line by line.

### Daft Punk, One More Time
The vocal on "One More Time" is deliberately over compressed and distorted.
Critics initially treated it as a mistake rather than a choice.

### Stardust, Music Sounds Better with You
The group released this single in 1998 and then essentially stopped, leaving one
of house music's most recognisable records as their only output.

### a-ha, Take on Me
The video used rotoscoping, tracing animation over live footage frame by frame.
The song had already been released twice and failed before the video carried it.

### Eurythmics, Sweet Dreams
"Sweet Dreams" was recorded on an eight track in a small London studio the duo
built themselves after their previous band collapsed.

### The Verve, Bitter Sweet Symphony
The orchestral loop was licensed from a Rolling Stones cover version, but the
band was judged to have used more than agreed and lost the songwriting royalties
for over twenty years.

### Pulp, Common People
"Common People" is a story about a wealthy student who wants to live poor as an
experience, and the narrator's growing anger at the idea.

### Soundgarden, Black Hole Sun
Chris Cornell has said the title came before the song and that the lyrics do not
mean anything specific, which has not stopped decades of interpretation.

### Pearl Jam, Alive
The verse of "Alive" tells a bleak story, but audiences turned the chorus into a
celebration, and the band came to accept the reinterpretation.

### Franz Ferdinand, Take Me Out
"Take Me Out" changes tempo about a minute in and never returns. The band has
described it as two separate songs stitched together.

### MGMT, Electric Feel
"Electric Feel" is in an unusual time feel for a pop single, and the band spent
years afterwards distancing themselves from their own biggest hits.

### Madonna, Hung Up
"Hung Up" is built on a sample from ABBA's "Gimme! Gimme! Gimme!". ABBA almost
never clear samples, and Madonna reportedly wrote to them personally.

### Kes, Savannah Grass
Kes is a Trinidadian band that plays soca with a rock lineup, and "Savannah
Grass" is named for the Queen's Park Savannah where carnival competitions are
staged.

### Panjabi MC, Mundian To Bach Ke
The track pairs a bhangra dhol pattern with the theme from the television series
Knight Rider, and a later version with Jay-Z took it worldwide.

### Amr Diab, Nour El Ein
"Nour El Ein", often known as "Habibi", was released in 1996 and became one of
the first Arabic pop records to chart widely in Europe.

### Rosalia, Malamente
"Malamente" from El Mal Querer announced Rosalia's approach of putting flamenco
structures inside contemporary production, and it drew criticism from flamenco
purists.

### Kavinsky, Nightcall
"Nightcall" was released in 2010 and became inseparable from the film Drive,
which used it over the opening titles.
